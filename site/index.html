Codex and Claude Code limits, made visible

OpenAI Codex and Anthropic’s Claude Code cap your usage twice: a limit over 5 hours and another over the week. Once it runs out you wait for the refill, and neither tool tells you clearly where you stand across several accounts. Codex Tracker answers three questions at a glance.

How much is left?

The remaining percentage is drawn in the taskbar icon. Hover it for both limits of the active account.

When does it reset?

The exact reset time of every limit, for every account, as a list or on a week calendar.

Which account should I use?

All your Codex and Claude Code accounts side by side, personal and work, each with the date of its last reading.

Everything about your quotas, nothing else

Your accounts at a glance

A ring for the active account, a bar for the others, and the age of every reading. Inactive accounts keep their last value; once their reset time passes they show as “probably refilled”.

Reset calendar and banked credits

Upcoming resets day by day, plus your Codex reset credits with their expiry. Get notified when a new credit arrives, and advised to use one before it expires unused.

Google Calendar and Gmail, in one click

Connect Google Calendar and every banked reset lands in your calendar with reminders, removed once you use it. An email from your own Gmail the day before a credit expires.

Reminders that fit

Before a reset, below 20, 10 or 5 %, before running out, or when another account may have refilled. Running low? It names the account to switch to. Windows notifications by default, copied for free to your phone with ntfy, even in Do Not Disturb; SMS, call or email with your own Twilio or SendGrid account.

For coding assistants

A local MCP server, off by default, lets your coding agent read quotas, plan around resets and change settings, with local confirmation for anything external. The same commands work on the command line: CodexTracker.exe --cli list_resets.

Quiet and native

Light or dark like Windows, English or French, starts with Windows, updates itself. Closing the window keeps it next to the clock.

Install in three steps

  1. Download and run the installer. No admin rights, nothing else to install.
  2. Use Codex or Claude Code as usual. The signed-in account appears by itself; other accounts are added when you open them.
  3. Keep the icon visible by dragging it out of the taskbar’s ^ overflow.

Prefer no installer? A portable ZIP is on the releases page. The executables are not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn on first launch.

Your data stays on your PC

Frequently asked questions

How do I see how much Codex usage I have left?

Install Codex Tracker and keep using Codex as usual. The tray icon shows the remaining percentage of the active account; hover it for the 5-hour and weekly limits and the next reset time.

When does my Codex weekly limit reset?

Each account has its own reset time. Codex Tracker reads it from Codex and shows the exact date and hour for every account, with an optional reminder.

Does it work with Claude Code?

Yes. The Claude desktop app is detected automatically. For Claude Code in a terminal, copy the status line setting offered in Settings → General → Claude Code.

What are Codex reset credits?

Codex can grant banked reset credits that refill a limit on demand and expire after a while. Codex Tracker lists them with their expiry, notifies you when one arrives, adds it to Google Calendar and advises using it before it is wasted.

Why is only the open account updated?

The tracker reads the active account in Codex and in Claude. Other accounts keep their last dated reading until you open them again.

Does it need to stay open?

For reminders, yes: it sits quietly in the taskbar and can start with Windows. The PC must be on.

Is it free?

Yes, free and open source under the MIT license. Only optional SMS, calls and SendGrid emails go through your own provider accounts and their prices.

Is it affiliated with OpenAI or Anthropic?

No. Codex Tracker is an independent project. Codex and Claude are trademarks of their respective owners.
